# Foil Academy

A separate, offline 3D browser game about wingfoiling. The game is listed in the root README and game hub; the other games remain separate.

## Play

Open `../foil-academy.html` in a current browser with WebGL. Everything is embedded: Three.js, procedural artwork, shaders, sound, controls, and simulation. No install, CDN, account, or network request is needed to play after the HTML loads. The build produces only `foil-academy.html`; the old duplicate `foil-school.html` has been removed. The existing save-data key is retained.

Location changes now rebuild the scene **inside the already loaded page**, with no new HTML request. The previous WebGL resources, audio and global input handlers are released first. Touch devices use a lower drawing resolution without multisample antialiasing to reduce GPU memory. This avoids URL-reload failures in restricted previews, but opening as a normal Safari webpage remains the most reliable option.

For an iPad, serve the file as a webpage rather than using the Files app's HTML preview:

1. Connect the laptop and iPad to the same Wi-Fi.
2. In the repository folder, run `python3 -m http.server 8000 --bind 0.0.0.0`.
3. In iPad Safari, open `http://YOUR-LAPTOP-IP:8000/foil-academy.html`.

You can also put the single HTML file on a static website. Saved lesson stars and personal records belong to the browser and site address. Sound begins after the first interaction and can be muted. Landscape gives the widest view, with responsive portrait controls available too.

Small, light keycaps show W/A/S/D around the body pad and arrow keys around the wing and travel pads. The body pad and wing pad support independent simultaneous pointers. Releasing the pad centers body input; wing position and trim remain where you leave them. Pointer cancellation, interrupted touches, focus loss, and hidden tabs release transient input. Hidden tabs pause the session.

Activity, discoveries/flight time and points stay in a narrow left rail. Speed, the wind compass and ride height share one compact panel on the right; the chart remains available in the top toolbar. Coaching and temporary notices share a slim strip below the controls; longer messages scroll inside that strip. Short windows use compact panels with scrolling where needed, keeping the center view and thumb controls clear. The `hud` browser check covers these bounds in sailing, swimming and walking at desktop, tablet, portrait-phone and short landscape sizes.

## Learn and explore

The nineteen courses are Taxiing, First flights, Touch-and-go, Sustained flight, Pumping, S-turns, Carving, Jibe, Foot switching, Tack, Wave riding, Downwind gliding, Popping, Frontside airs, Backside airs, Air tacks, Aerial 360s, Backflips, and Frontflips. Turns and carving precede full transitions; straight jumps precede aerial rotations. Each course has a distinct measured objective, including settled exits and controlled landings. All courses are available immediately. Completing a course earns one star; at most two falls earns two; no falls earns three. Stable course IDs preserve stars if the order changes.

The opening screen offers an academy location selector, defaulting to **Academy Bay**. Keyboard focus starts on the main start button. The chosen site carries through courses and **Free Foiling**, the last option in the course list.

Flight earns points continuously, with a multiplier that grows from 1× to 2.5× over a long uninterrupted run. Foiling jibes, tacks, and buoy gates add bonuses. A touchdown ends the current flight streak but keeps earned points. The Conditions menu is available from the top bar and pause screen during lessons, free foiling and exploration. It controls wind strength (0–28 knots), wind direction, gusts, wave height (0–1.2 m crest to trough), spacing (4–80 m), choppiness, wave travel direction, cloud cover and sunlight. Apply keeps the current position, flight log and lesson progress; Cancel or Escape discards edits. Each new lesson starts with its teaching conditions.

Free Foiling starts with **Lively ripples**: approximately 16 cm high, short, quick waves with crossing wavelets. Other presets offer an easy breeze, long rolling swell, overcast chop, short crossed bay chop, and glassy calm. Shorter spacing also shortens the period using deep-water wave scaling. Choppiness mixes extra crossing wave trains within the chosen height bound. Physics, visible displacement, wake and foil clearance use the same component table and parameters. Height is an approximate upper crest-to-trough envelope, not a calibrated significant wave height. Wind comes *from* its bearing; waves travel *toward* theirs. Free Foiling also offers coached or direct controls.

The bay now includes a larger surrounding coastline, layered rock and vegetation, a lighthouse, harbor buildings, watchtower, ruined castle headland, and chalk cliffs. Open the ⌖ bay chart to choose a landmark: its true bearing and distance appear beneath the wind compass. Shore labels identify visible references, and the chart shows north, the rider, the launch buoy, and the next flight gate.

The rider uses shaped anatomical meshes with bent joints, textured neoprene, an impact vest, helmet, modeled hands, and boots. Arm and leg lengths remain fixed as the hands track the wing handles. Walking blends into jogging and running, with longer strides, more knee lift and bent arms. Full forward stick reaches 5.4 m/s; a light push walks. Holding Up smoothly builds speed after a short walking start; repeated taps also select faster paces. Acceleration uses elapsed time, independent of operating-system key repeat. The arms counter-swing from the shoulders with forward-bending elbows and relaxed fists. Facial features, vest stitching and fittings, hand joints and boot seams add detail without changing the rider’s proportions. Release brakes to a stop within about half a metre at full speed. Reverse walking remains slow. Swimming has separate reach, catch, pull, and recovery phases, open palms, shoulder-led body roll, side breathing, and a small six-beat flutter kick. A separate stroke clock keeps the cadence natural at higher travel speeds, and the torso stays at the waterline when blending into treading or backward sculling. A coiled board leash connects the ankle cuff to the tail plug, and the wing leash connects the wrist cuff to the wing attachment. Both cords update their geometry between those attachment points.

The water uses nearby and distant meshes, multi-scale surface normals, Fresnel reflection, a planar reflection pass, depth-dependent color, shoreline foam, and a wake that follows the actual path through turns. Reflection resolution is reduced on touch devices, scenery is instanced, and static rider details are batched. This is procedural real-time art; it does not use photographic human scans or a full fluid simulation.

Left input banks and leans left, and turns left in the chase view. While swimming, walking or riding, looking is independent of movement: WASD or the left joystick changes camera yaw and pitch, while arrows or the right touch pad move and turn. Releasing the look input smoothly returns the view to the travel heading. Both touch pads work simultaneously. The camera stops before masonry and boulders.

The live coach explains power loss, oversheeting, excessive foil height, hand transfers, and pumping. The compass shows heading relative to true wind, the upwind no-go sector, apparent wind direction, and the next gate. The height meter highlights the target riding band. A field guide explains the controls and learning concepts in the game.

## Swim and explore Castle Head

Jump off at any point while sailing with **E** or **Jump off**. The board and wing remain afloat. You initially tow them; **Space** or **Leave board** lets you swim freely. Swim within 4 m to take the leash again, or within 3.2 m and choose **Climb aboard** to return to sailing. Stand and sheet in again after boarding. Points and lesson progress are retained. Holding Up builds from about 2.2 to 3.2 m/s for free swimming, or about 1.4 to 2.0 m/s while towing. The right joystick gives proportional speed, with brief acceleration and deceleration instead of instant sliding. Hand entries produce small splashes and a narrow wake. These speeds are gameplay tuning, not a prediction of real swimming performance.

The freestyle stroke uses a shoulder-width entry, a submerged catch and pull, and an elbow-led recovery with relaxed hands, following [U.S. Masters Swimming’s technique guide](https://www.usms.org/fitness-and-training/articles-and-videos/articles/the-right-and-wrong-time-to-have-high-elbows-on-freestyle). Looking from below the swimmer now puts the camera in blue-green water: warm colors fade, distant objects become hazy, and the rippled surface has a brighter refracted sky window overhead. The effect follows wave height and fades away on resurfacing. Bay/lake water is greener and less clear than Maui; these are visual approximations, informed by [NOAA’s light and color reference](https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/edu/materials/light-and-color-fact-sheet.pdf), not measured water-quality data.

Low beaches and the landing below **Castle Head** automatically switch from swimming to walking. The board stays in the water when you go ashore. Choose Castle Head on the **⌖** chart, approach the sheltered landing, and follow the stone steps. The courtyard, sea gate, mosaic hall, and signal tower contain four discoveries worth 75 points each. Masonry walls and the well block walking; steps and floors have matching collision surfaces. Walk back down to the landing and into the water to retrieve the board. In water and on land, arrows or the right joystick move and turn, while WASD or the left joystick looks around and recenters on release.

Shoreline also has one small pedalboat with two people sitting side by side, animated pedals and a paddle wheel. It follows a slow, continuous circuit in open water; hitting it while sailing triggers the usual fall and swim recovery. This reflects [Shoreline Lake’s pedalboat activities](https://m.shorelinelake.com/paddle_days.html).

## Bonus horse rides

Clover or Willow waits near the shore access at each location, on open ground beside the water / launch area, with a smaller named navigation label above it. Labels use short leader lines and avoid covering nearby horses or the player. Shoreline’s horse waits on the small beach-side lawn; Coyote’s is in the waterfront parking lot beside the central grey-gravel staging triangle; Third’s is beside the lower concrete ramp. In Academy Bay, Willow is just inland of the water landing, about 24 m from the dock. Horses also appear on the navigation chart. Walk close and use **H / Ride**. The board stays in the water. The right joystick steers and controls pace; small forward input walks, then progressively stronger input trots, canters and gallops. Arrow keys work too. WASD looks around without steering the horse. Release to brake, pull back to reverse slowly, and stop before **H / Dismount** or **E**. The left pad independently looks around and recenters when released. **Space / Jump** makes the horse leap over low barriers or onto parked cars; land before jumping again. Standing jumps also move forward or backward with the arrows/right joystick; a running jump retains its greater distance. The full leg stride stops at car edges, and unsafe partial landings settle on clear footing. Car roof and hood support match the rendered heights. Tall walls and water still stop travel. Jump height is playful game tuning.

The mane is rooted in the sampled neck surface and moves with it. The saddle pad and leather follow the rib cage, with a narrower seat beneath the rider’s thighs and fitted stirrups. Saddle shaping and rider balance use the [British Horse Society’s tack guidance](https://www.bhs.org.uk/go-riding-and-learn/stable-mates/tack-fit/) as a visual reference.

The horse uses one continuous skinned surface from the torso through the shoulders, upper arms, thighs and gaskins. A smaller, sloping croup and fuller upper legs replace the bulbous rump / thin separate limbs. Muscle blends across the moving joints instead of disappearing into a rigid torso. The model also has a shaped neck, longer muzzle, eyes, nostrils, mane, tail, saddle, stirrups, bridle and reins. The rider sits over the saddle with bent knees, heels down and relaxed elbows. Hooves follow the ground with fixed-length leg joints. Walk, trot, canter and gallop have distinct footfall timing, body motion and hoof sounds. Front legs have separate shoulder, elbow, carpal knee and fetlock joints; hind legs have separate hip, forward-bending stifle, backward-pointing hock and fetlock joints, following [University of Calgary veterinary anatomy diagrams](https://vet.ucalgary.ca/summercamps/anatomy/tuesday/equine). Stairs automatically slow the horse to a walk. Collision samples the whole animal and sweeps its movement to prevent passing through walls, fences, parked cars, tree trunks, cliffs or water. Walking collision follows the horse’s rounded body and head as it moves and turns, while leaving room to approach and mount. Small stair risers are traversable; the horse waits where you dismount. Horse exploration does not earn foil-flight credit.

These are procedural animated models and simplified riding controls, not scanned horses or an equestrian simulator. Footfall references: [Penn State / Extension gait chart](https://horses.extension.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/gaits-of-the-ho_53762938.pdf), [University of Minnesota conformation guide](https://extension.umn.edu/agriculture/animals-and-livestock/horse/conformation-of-the-horse), and [New Vocations horse profile photographs](https://www.newvocations.org/horses), [Novara Park side/front/hind conformation photos](https://www.novarapark.co.nz/the-foxes/conformation/), [University of Georgia anatomy and balance photographs](https://fieldreport.caes.uga.edu/publications/B1400/evaluating-horse-conformation/), and Muybridge’s [walking](https://www.nga.gov/artworks/166999-plate-number-580-annie-g-walking-saddled), [cantering](https://www.nga.gov/artworks/167036-plate-number-618-middleton-cantering-saddled) and [galloping](https://www.nga.gov/artworks/167045-plate-number-627-annie-g-galloping) plates. Browser validation samples 200 poses for fixed bone lengths, planted hooves, bounded skin deformation and rider seating, and captures four phases per gait plus front/rear views.

## Read the wind and avoid the shallows

The compass number is **true wind**, while the wing panel reports usable drive and its limiting cause. Green compass arcs mark takeoff reaches near 90°. Begin around 65–70% trim; oversheeting, the upwind no-go zone, a hand transfer, deep downwind sailing, and actual lulls have separate feedback. Wing transfers use a simplified aerodynamic load estimate based on apparent wind (true wind plus board motion), wing exposure and actual trim. They do not use a fixed power cutoff. A slow, head-to-wind rider can switch with a high power setting when the wing feathers; a strongly loaded or backwinded wing still needs unloading. The wing pad changes its outline under load; the main status asks you to ease if a transfer is blocked. Tiny secondary text below the pad is omitted for readability. An overhead wing produces slightly less forward drive than a lowered wing at equal trim, with a stronger difference when sailing close-hauled. Lowering the wing to the powered side improves horizontal drive; the wrong side still backwinds. Sheeting visibly changes the canopy angle. Oversheeting starts at 90%, consistently in the drive curve and status display. A sheeted-in wing facing directly into the wind can push the board slowly astern (limited to 1.5 m/s); easing removes that force. A sheeted-in wing catches stronger pressure from astern even directly downwind; drive diminishes as the board approaches the wind speed, and becomes braking if it outruns the wind. Apparent wind includes sideways drift. A sheeted-in wing on the wrong side produces braking and downwind sideslip; fully easing removes those forces. Wind socks and flags stream downwind using the same world wind direction as the force model. A fall resets wing power to zero. Wipeouts carry the rider through the air using forward momentum and the direction of lost balance, then end in the water. The rider remains swimming until you press Enter / Climb aboard near the board; stand up and sheet in again to sail. Earned points are preserved. Body keys keep working after using the wing pad.

**UNDER FOIL** shows clearance below the lowest part of the submerged equipment. Collision tests sweep the board and foil, including banked wingtips, across the same terrain triangles used for the visible islands and coastline. Underwater slopes, shoreline rocks, and the landing pier are included. A warning anticipates shallow water ahead; a strike causes a fall into the water; tow the equipment clear of the shallows before climbing aboard. This check uses ride height, pitch, roll, and the moving water surface rather than a circular shoreline approximation.

Every pump press starts charging, including during recovery from the previous stroke. Release near the green band, then press again immediately for the next stroke. Recovery and stamina still affect the strength of a stroke; rapid taps do not create useful pumping.

## Physics and learning scope

The fixed-step simulation includes wind-relative heading, changing wind speed, apparent wind, a simplified wing power curve with oversheeting, hull versus foil drag, speed-squared foil lift, fore-and-aft pitch control, heave damping, surface ventilation, touchdown, and wave-induced clearance changes. Pumping requires a timed compression/release with a recovery interval and energy budget. Jibes and tacks count only when the rider crosses the correct wind axis, stays on foil, transfers the wing, and exits on the opposite reach.

Back-foot pressure (**S** or pad down) shifts the rider aft, raises the nose, and increases foil lift at speed. Front-foot pressure (**W** or pad up) lowers the nose and reduces lift, allowing the foil to descend. Pitch alone cannot lift a stationary board. Positive simulation pitch means nose up; the shared equipment transform converts it to negative Three.js X rotation because the board's nose points along local +Z. Rendering and seabed collision checks use this same transform, including the pump kick.

This is a forgiving educational model, not a calibrated equipment or CFD simulator. The coefficients favor readable, recoverable gameplay. Coached mode adds damping and adjusts the neutral foil pitch for speed after takeoff, so centering the pad helps settle the board. The player still supplies takeoff pressure, steering, wing trim, and wave/height corrections. Direct mode retains unassisted pitch control. Wing transfers and foot switches are separate controls; the game does not model every real hand or foot technique. Real-world instruction remains valuable for safe launching, equipment handling, and movement technique.

The learning progression was informed by the [RYA wing courses](https://www.rya.org.uk/training/wing-courses/) and [wing scheme syllabus](https://www.rya.org.uk/media/ur5nmnk2/9ba_rya_wing_scheme_syllabus_pdf.pdf). Wing handling references include [Ozone's learning series](https://ozonekites.com/learn-to-wingfoil-video-series/) and [wing manual](https://ozonekites.com/wing-manual/). The procedural crawl animation also references [Swim England’s front-crawl technique guide](https://www.swimming.org/masters/improving-front-crawl-technique/). These organizations have not reviewed or endorsed the game.

The castle's staggered masonry, door jambs, tower openings and larger fallen stones share their geometry with collision checks. Boulders use their rendered rotation. The player can walk through the sea gate, hall arch and all three tower doorways, with solid walls around those openings. Beveled, textured stone, arrow slits, courtyard paving and broken masonry add detail without external assets.

The board is a procedural interpretation of the [Naish Hover Wing Foil Carbon Ultra](https://www.naish.com/products/s26-hover-wing-foil-carbon-ultra), using its curved outline, tapered tail, rocker, a clean, very light grey textured deck. Decorative curved stripes have been removed. The wing uses [Cabrinha Mantis product photos](https://www.mackiteboarding.com/2025-cabrinha-mantis-wing/) as its visual reference: a tapered dark leading edge, low dihedral, teal panels, visibility windows and a curved central strut. The canopy billows outward away from the rider. It has closed leading-edge ends, reinforced corners, a finite chord at each tip and continuous seam geometry. Portrait camera framing includes the complete wing.


## Real locations and other riders

The five real sites alongside Academy Bay are Shoreline Park Lake, 3rd Avenue (starting beside Lower Launch), Coyote Point Beach, Crissy Field South Beach, and Kahului Harbor, Maui (Ho‘aloha Park). All six support lessons and Free Foiling. Shorelines, major building footprints, paths, parking areas and planting zones come from OpenStreetMap. USGS 3DEP bare-earth elevations shape the land and distant hills without vertical exaggeration. Local elevation samples are 8–12 m apart; regional samples are about 110 m apart. Maui uses a separate regional grid extending to Haleakalā and the West Maui mountains. Bay water uses an approximate 0.97 m NAVD88 reference, and the lake uses the sampled 2.98 m level. Hawaiian land uses the USGS local mean-sea-level datum, with a zero-meter game water level. Tides and underwater bathymetry are modeled rather than surveyed.

Site defaults use wind **from WNW (292.5°)** at Coyote and Third Avenue, **W (270°)** at Crissy, **NNW (337.5°)** at Shoreline, and **NE (45°)** trade winds at Kahului. Shoreline uses nearby Moffett observations as a regional proxy, not a surveyed wind climatology for the lake. Conditions can override every default. Switching locations for Free Foiling updates the wind and wave direction; initial lesson headings choose a clear practice reach. Wave-riding courses align their waves with the practice reach, and downwind gliding uses downwind travel.

Parking spaces follow mapped lot outlines and drive aisles instead of an arbitrary rectangle. Shoreline includes curved parking rows, planted islands and trees screening the lot. Coyote includes its waterfront lots, roads, North Shoreview pump station, and densely packed riprap; both mapped beaches remain clear of rocks. Third includes the lower concrete launch ramp, separate rescue jet-ski dock, turf pads, water-facing benches, low chain-link parking enclosure with access gaps, and a representative lesson RV. Arbor’s daytime upper-launch, north and wide camera views show an open grassy bank between the launches, with low hills up to the driving range and trees farther inland. A dedicated exclusion zone removes shoreline trees and shrubs there; mapped grass, greens and USGS elevations remain. The entire golf-course waterfront path is open without a guardrail or storm-surge wall. A continuous asphalt surface uses an approximate smooth 3.15 m grade independent of coarse terrain bumps; the adjoining terrain and collision match it, and riprap / plants stay clear of the paving. The Cabrinha flag is yellow with a worn fly edge, following the user's local observation; the older indexed photo shows a white version. A one-metre terrain patch resolves the steep bank beside the lower ramp. Gilead buildings have varied masses, a lower meeting annex, parking garage and four white water tanks located from aerial imagery. Unverified architectural details and tank heights remain approximate.

Photo-guided details include the Shoreline boathouse, docks, boats, screened parking and SolarBee water circulator; Coyote's wooded knoll, continuous promenade, concrete access ramps, rock armor, Boardsports building, Meta campus and SFO; 3rd Avenue's golf course, Gilead campus, Bay Trail and continuous San Mateo Bridge with both mapped approaches; and Crissy's dunes, cypress groves, Presidio hangars, Warming Hut and distant landmarks. Full tree canopies replace sparse leaf scatter. Open beaches, the café lawn and fairways remain clear. Shoreline boulders support walking over them, while buildings still block the player. Coyote’s Meta-to-beach promenade has its own continuous, horizontally flat grade, with asphalt paving, a pale concrete apron, low trapezoidal seat-wall, lights and concrete water-access ramps. The terrain is cut beneath that engineered surface so coarse elevation samples cannot poke through it; walking and foil collision use the same paving and ramp heights. Small grass and tree positions, architectural details and the SolarBee mooring are approximations informed by photos, not surveyed replicas. The boathouse reflects its familiar photographed form rather than a live construction update.

Kahului follows the mapped harbor coastline and breakwaters. Ho‘aloha includes two open canoe hales with racks, outrigger canoes, a six-person paddling crew with articulated arms and alternating-side catch/pull/recovery strokes on a continuous nearshore loop, coconut palms, broadleaf trees and naupaka with beach-access gaps. The paved L-shaped park lot, grass overflow area and Puʻunēnē Avenue parking sit behind the beach. A chain-link boundary separates the public side from cargo yards. Fuel tanks, container rows, representative moored cargo and cruise ships, navigation buoys and wind indicators complete the harbor view. Quays, ship hulls, the fence and shoreline participate in collision; a horse waits near the beach. Ships, buoy positions, vegetation placement and small site details are illustrative, not a live port or navigation chart.

Shoreline’s play boat is the **Mountain View sailing scow**. The city’s replacement plan retains its original footprint, shape and height; the game recreates that familiar form with two masts, rigging, a wheel and a usable boarding ramp. It does not assert that construction is finished. Verified background additions include Shoreline Amphitheatre, Google Bay View’s canopy roofs and restored Hangar One; the Hilton and Courtyard beyond Coyote Point; and Fort Mason’s piers, Transamerica Pyramid, Salesforce Tower and selected downtown buildings at Crissy. Hotel heights use photo-guided estimates where OSM tags conflict with their visible floor counts. No random buildings were added to fill the coast. Coyote’s beach-side parking edge includes low planting beds and timber/wire fencing based on county completion photos.

Conditions includes **Other foilers** (0–25) and **Foiler experience** (beginner/expert ratio). Both preferences persist. Each visit gets a new random seed. The riders use the player's wind, foil lift, pumping, fall and recovery model, with different control skill. They unload and smoothly transfer their wings through turns; beginners taxi, try flight and fall, while experts pump and sustain flight. Nearby riders steer away instead of disappearing. Their wings use the same swept, inflated planform as the player's wing, with shared instanced geometry to keep rendering affordable. There is no separate rigid-body collision simulation between riders.

Sparse windsocks provide shore references at every site; the Shoreline boathouse has an animated US flag. All indicate where the air is blowing toward, whereas the compass wind bearing indicates where it comes from.

## Additional shoreline and harbor reference pass

Kahului uses a single plain sand overlay above the park ground, with separated surface layers to avoid grass/sand depth flicker. Three small inner-beach basalt groins and a low rock shelf were traced from an [Esri World Imagery aerial](https://server.arcgisonline.com/ArcGIS/rest/services/World_Imagery/MapServer), checked against the [county Hoaloha site survey](https://ssfm.konveio.com/sites/ssfm.konveio.com/files/u2/Hoaloha%20Park%20Adaptation%20Plan%20-%20Site%20Assessment.pdf) and [University of Hawaiʻi shoreline description](https://www.soest.hawaii.edu/crc/resources-2/historical-mosaics/kahului/). Their rocks share the visible and walkable support model; overlapping low stones can be scrambled over. The large breakwaters have a dense armor mantle. Cargo yards contain varied container stacks with ribs and doors; quays extend below the water. Cruise-ship detail draws from [NCL's Pride of America imagery and deck layouts](https://www.ncl.com/cruise-ships/pride-of-america/deck-plans): rounded hull, stacked balconies, forward bridge, covered lifeboats, pool deck, funnel and radar domes. The moored vessel is a representative, compressed game model, not a precise ship replica or live port inventory.

The Golden Gate now includes its complete suspension and approach structures, hangers on both side spans, anchorages, open approach supports, and the arch above Fort Point. The [bridge district's dimensions](https://www.goldengate.org/bridge/history-research/statistics-data/design-construction-stats/) set the span lengths; [Crissy waterfront photographs](https://inspiredimperfection.com/adventures/crissy-field-golden-gate-promenade/) guide the arch and fort detail. Approach ends meet the game's terrain; their grades remain approximations. Thin distant hangers use continuous screen-space lines to avoid dotted silhouettes.

Shoreline's stored dinghies and clean lawn use [September 2025 lakeside photographs](https://www.mv-voice.com/food/2025/10/14/shoreline-lake-cafe-looks-to-operate-from-food-truck-during-construction/). The boathouse represents its familiar operating appearance rather than temporary construction. Coyote's pale concrete and timber beach access use [OMGstudio's completed promenade photos](https://www.omgstudio.com/coyote-point). Third Avenue keeps the open, low golf-course banks and adds dark slatted benches based on [Arbor Studios launch photos](https://arbor-studios.com/ThirdAveKiteboarding/); the golf edge has no new tree row or storm wall.

The upwind force adjustment follows [SROKA's close-hauled technique guide](https://srokacompany.com/en/blogs/news/3-tips-for-going-upwind-on-a-wing-foil). [RYA's foiling instructor guide](https://www.rya.org.uk/media/sqqls32n/29d_sailfoil_instructor_guide.pdf) describes the loss of rig pressure when sailing too far downwind. Broad reaches retain better sustained speed than heading directly downwind. Exact force multipliers, the 90% oversheet threshold and reverse-speed limit are accessible game tuning, not universal equipment measurements.

The invented parking fence is removed, and the concrete access at the west corner of the waterfront lot follows the aerial. The public path to Magic Mountain is checked by walking it with the same collision model used by the player. The playground recreates the familiar blue castle, steel tube slide, purple dragons, green swing frames and climbing arch from [Miracle Playsystems' project photographs](https://www.miracleplaysystems.com/magic-mountain-playground) and the [county park map](https://www.smcgov.org/media/149824/download?inline=).
